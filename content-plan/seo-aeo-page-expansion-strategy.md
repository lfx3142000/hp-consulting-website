# SEO/AEO Page Expansion Strategy

## Bottom line

Yes, the site should have more pages, but only when each page has a distinct visitor intent, a clear answer, useful expert perspective, and a conversion path.

The goal is not to create one page for every keyword variation. The goal is to build a structured library that helps radiation safety visitors answer real questions and then submit a request, question, download, or signup if they want more.

## Why more pages are justified

The current site has broad service pages and several useful blog posts. That is a good start, but it leaves search opportunity on the table because visitors search in several different ways:

1. By service: radiation safety consultant, RSO consulting, radioactive material license consultant.
2. By problem: preparing for NRC inspection, license amendment, annual review, missing records.
3. By document: NRC Form 313, radiation safety manual, survey record, training record, audit checklist.
4. By facility type: nuclear medicine, university lab, biotech, radiopharmaceutical, industrial gauge user.
5. By jurisdiction/location: Massachusetts, New England, NRC, Agreement State.
6. By definition: restricted area, controlled area, ALARA, occupational dose limit, package receipt survey.
7. By template: audit checklist, package receipt checklist, survey log, decay-in-storage log.

AEO also favors pages that provide clean, direct answers to specific questions. However, pages should not be fragmented so much that they become thin, repetitive, or doorway-like.

## Page expansion rule

Create a new page only if it passes all six tests:

1. Distinct intent: the page answers a materially different question or serves a different visitor type.
2. Search value: the page targets a query, question, or cluster people are likely to search.
3. Unique expertise: the page includes practical judgment, examples, checklists, or decision criteria not found in generic summaries.
4. Conversion path: the page links to a request form, question form, free resource, or signup.
5. Internal fit: the page belongs to a topic hub and links to related pages.
6. Non-duplication: the page does not substantially repeat an existing page.

If a topic fails these tests, add it as an FAQ section or subsection on an existing page instead of creating a new URL.

## What not to do

Do not create:

- Dozens of city pages with the same copy.
- Near-duplicate pages for every keyword variation.
- Thin glossary pages with no internal links or practical examples.
- AI-generated commodity summaries without expert judgment.
- Pages that exist only to funnel users to another page.
- Pages that promise availability or response times.

## AEO-specific planning notes

AEO pages should be written for extraction and usefulness, but not in a robotic format. Each page should answer a question directly near the top, then provide enough practical context that the page remains useful if a user clicks through from an AI summary.

Good AEO page features:

- Direct answer block.
- Short definitions.
- Tables or checklists.
- FAQ section.
- Clear source/regulatory notes.
- Author/expertise note.
- Internal links.
- Download or question CTA.

## Measurement rules for page expansion

Add more pages when Search Console or form data shows:

- Rising impressions for a topic.
- Clicks to a service or article page.
- Submitted questions around a topic.
- Free resource downloads.
- Project inquiries in a category.

Pause or merge pages when:

- Two pages serve the same intent.
- A page receives impressions but no clicks after title/meta revisions.
- A page receives traffic but no engagement.
- A topic does not match the future business direction.
