# Radioactive Material License Reciprocity in New England

**Direct answer:** A radioactive material license issued by the NRC or an Agreement State does not automatically authorize use or storage of licensed material in another jurisdiction. Temporary work may be possible through reciprocity, but the receiving regulator's requirements must be satisfied **before** work begins. All six New England states—Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont—are NRC Agreement States for covered radioactive materials.

## Who regulates temporary work?
For temporary licensed activities within an Agreement State's jurisdiction, consult that **state radiation control program** for its reciprocity application, fees, notification lead time, permissible activities and duration. NRC-licensed entities and licensees of other Agreement States must follow the receiving state's rules. NRC may retain authority over certain facilities, federal jurisdictions, and activities even within Agreement States.

For work in **NRC jurisdiction** by a qualifying Agreement State specific licensee, **10 CFR 150.20** provides a general license subject to conditions. It covers non-Agreement States, certain areas of exclusive federal jurisdiction, and offshore waters.

## NRC Form 241 and timing
The NRC generally requires the Agreement State licensee to submit **NRC Form 241**, a copy of its current license, and the appropriate fee to the NRC regional office at least **three calendar days** before the first proposed activity in a calendar year. In unusual circumstances, the NRC Regional Administrator **may** waive the three-day timing requirement; do not assume an emergency automatically waives it. Activities under the NRC general license are generally limited to **180 days per calendar year**, except for offshore waters. Days of material storage in NRC jurisdiction also count. Verify the current NRC guidance and license conditions.

## Checklist before interstate work
1. Confirm the home license is active and authorizes the material, activity and temporary location.
2. Identify the actual regulator at the destination, including whether the site is in exclusive federal jurisdiction.
3. Obtain the receiving state's reciprocity authorization or complete NRC Form 241 as applicable.
4. Verify notice periods, fees, day limits, safety/security controls, transport requirements and site-specific license conditions.
5. Retain approvals and document days of use **and storage**.

## Frequently asked questions
**Can a Massachusetts licensee work temporarily in New Hampshire without a new specific license?** Potentially, through **New Hampshire's** reciprocity process. New Hampshire is an Agreement State; do not automatically submit NRC Form 241 for ordinary state-jurisdiction work.

**Does NRC Form 241 authorize work in every Agreement State?** No. NRC Form 241 addresses eligible work in NRC jurisdiction. Each receiving Agreement State establishes its own reciprocity provisions.

**Are all federal properties NRC jurisdiction?** Not necessarily. Confirm whether the particular location is an area of exclusive federal jurisdiction and which regulator controls the planned activity.
