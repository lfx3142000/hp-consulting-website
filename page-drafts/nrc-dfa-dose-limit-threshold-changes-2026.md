# NRC Proposes Changes to Declared Pregnant Worker Dose Limit Requirements

As part of the proposed ALARA removal rule (NRC-2025-1140), the NRC has proposed revisions to how dose limits apply to declared pregnant workers (DPWs). These changes directly affect radiation protection programs at hospitals, clinics, nuclear facilities, industrial radiography companies, and any NRC or Agreement State licensee who employs workers who may become pregnant.

## Current Requirements

Under 10 CFR 20.1208, a declared pregnant worker — defined as a woman who has voluntarily informed her employer of her pregnancy in writing — is subject to a dose limit of 5 mSv (500 mrem) to the embryo/fetus over the entire gestation period. The regulation also requires that dose be kept ALARA throughout the pregnancy.

## What the NRC Is Proposing

The proposed rule (NRC-2025-1140) would:

**Remove the ALARA reference from DPW dose provisions** — The proposal eliminates the regulatory requirement that embryo/fetus dose be maintained ALARA, replacing it with reliance on the numerical dose limit alone. This aligns with the broader ALARA removal effort across Part 20.

**Clarify the dose limit basis** — The NRC is clarifying that the 5 mSv limit applies to the total dose to the embryo/fetus during the entire declared pregnancy, with updated guidance on how to account for doses accumulated before declaration.

**Updated caregiver and comforter provisions** — The proposed rule also revises the dose provisions for members of the public who serve as caregivers to radiation therapy patients, which may intersect with DPW policy in clinical settings.

## Implications for Radiation Safety Programs

Licensees should review and potentially update the following program elements:

- Written policies for DPW declaration and counseling
- Dosimetry assignment and monitoring protocols for declared workers
- Radiation Safety Program (RSP) language referencing ALARA requirements for DPWs
- RSO training materials and worker briefings
- Procedures for calculating and tracking fetal dose

Note: Even if the ALARA regulatory requirement is removed, NRC guidance (and many facility policies) will likely still encourage ALARA practices as a best practice.

## Why This Change Is Being Proposed

The NRC has concluded that the numerical dose limit provides sufficient protection for the embryo/fetus and that the ALARA requirement in this context creates regulatory redundancy. The proposed change is intended to simplify compliance while maintaining the same level of radiological protection.

## Agreement State Implications

Agreement States are expected to adopt compatible provisions. Facilities in Agreement States should monitor their state radiation control program for corresponding regulatory updates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does this mean we no longer need to keep DPW doses ALARA?**
The regulatory requirement to keep embryo/fetus dose ALARA would be removed if this rule is finalized, but NRC guidance and facility best practices will still recommend ALARA approaches. Advantage Health Physics recommends maintaining ALARA as an institutional standard.

**Do we need to update our written DPW declaration policy?**
Yes, once the final rule is published, your Radiation Safety Program documents should be reviewed and updated to remove references to the ALARA regulatory requirement for DPWs and to reflect the updated rule language.

**How do we calculate fetal dose if a worker declared mid-pregnancy?**
Dose accumulated before declaration generally counts toward the 5 mSv limit. Advantage Health Physics can assist with retrospective dose calculations and documentation.
