# Package Receipt Survey Requirements for Radioactive Material

**Direct answer:** Under **10 CFR 20.1906**, incoming package monitoring depends on the package's radioactive labeling, radioactive material form, quantity relative to the Type A limit, and evidence of damage. **Not every package requires both an external radiation survey and a removable-contamination wipe test.** Written safe-opening procedures and any additional license conditions must be followed.

## Which incoming packages require monitoring?
- **Removable contamination:** Under 10 CFR 20.1906(b)(1), monitor the external surfaces of a package bearing a Radioactive White-I, Yellow-II, or Yellow-III label, **unless** it contains only radioactive material in gas form or **special form** as defined in 10 CFR 71.4.
- **External radiation levels:** Under 10 CFR 20.1906(b)(2), monitor the external surfaces of a labeled package **unless** its radioactive material quantity is less than or equal to the applicable **Type A** quantity.
- **Degraded packages:** Under 10 CFR 20.1906(b)(3), monitor **both contamination and radiation levels** on any package known to contain radioactive material when there is evidence of degraded package integrity, such as crushing, wetness or damage.

License conditions, facility procedures, or applicable Agreement State regulations may require additional monitoring. NRC's portable-gauge interpretation also addresses a special-form source transported in a licensee-owned vehicle under 20.1906(f).

## When must monitoring be completed?
Perform monitoring required by 20.1906(b) as soon as practical, but no later than **three hours after receipt** at the licensee's facility during normal working hours, or **three hours from the start of the next working day** when received after working hours (20.1906(c)).

## What happens when a limit is exceeded?
Under **10 CFR 20.1906(d)**, immediately notify the final delivery carrier and the NRC Headquarters Operations Center when removable surface contamination exceeds the applicable **10 CFR 71.87(i)** limit or external radiation levels exceed the applicable **10 CFR 71.47** limit. Do not use a single fixed dpm/100 cm² value without accounting for the controlling transport limits, radionuclide category and measurement method. Preserve readings, isolate suspect packages as appropriate, and follow the facility's response procedures. Agreement State licensees should verify applicable state reporting obligations.

## Package opening and documentation
10 CFR 20.1906(e) requires written procedures for safely opening packages and requires those procedures to be followed, including special instructions. Document the receipt, required monitoring results, instrument identification, anomalies, notifications and corrective actions under the applicable recordkeeping requirements and license commitments.

## FAQs
**Must every White-I package be wiped?** No. The special-form and gas exceptions in 20.1906(b)(1) may apply; a White-I label by itself does not remove those exceptions.

**Is a dose-rate survey required for every Yellow-II or Yellow-III package?** Section 20.1906(b)(2) uses the **Type A quantity** criterion, not a Yellow-II/III-only rule. Follow any more restrictive license conditions.

**Does a recent sealed-source leak test replace package receipt monitoring?** No. Package receipt monitoring concerns the **package's external surface** and follows 20.1906; sealed-source leak testing is a separate requirement.
