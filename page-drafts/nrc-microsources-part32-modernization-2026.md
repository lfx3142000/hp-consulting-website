# NRC Proposes Modernization of 10 CFR Part 32 Microsource Rules

The NRC has proposed revisions to 10 CFR Part 32 that would modernize the licensing framework for microsources — small sealed radioactive sources used in a variety of industrial and commercial applications. The proposed rule aims to reduce unnecessary regulatory burden while maintaining safety standards.

## What Are Microsources?

Microsources are sealed radioactive sources containing small quantities of radioactive material, typically used in:

- Electron capture detectors (ECDs) in gas chromatography equipment
- Static elimination devices
- Certain research and industrial instruments

Under current regulations, many of these devices are subject to general license requirements under 10 CFR Part 31, but the proposed Part 32 changes would update the manufacturing and distribution controls upstream.

## Key Proposed Changes

**Updated activity thresholds** — The NRC is reviewing whether current activity limits for microsource general licenses remain appropriate given modern usage and updated dose modeling.

**Streamlined transfer and distribution rules** — Manufacturers and distributors would face updated requirements for labeling, record-keeping, and reporting when supplying microsource devices to generally licensed end users.

**Alignment with international standards** — The proposed changes seek to align U.S. requirements more closely with IAEA guidance on exempt sources and source categorization.

**Revised labeling requirements** — Devices containing microsources would require updated labeling to reflect current regulatory language and contact information.

## Why This Matters for Regulated Entities

Businesses that manufacture, distribute, or use devices containing microsources should monitor this rulemaking closely. Changes to Part 32 distribution controls can affect:

- Device registration and transfer documentation requirements
- Labeling compliance on existing inventory
- Reporting obligations to the NRC upon device transfer or disposal
- State compatibility requirements for Agreement States

## Timeline

The NRC published an advance notice of proposed rulemaking (ANPR) and is currently in the proposed rule development phase. A proposed rule is expected to be published in the Federal Register for public comment. Final rule implementation is anticipated on a multi-year timeline.

## Agreement State Compatibility

Agreement States are expected to adopt compatible regulations following NRC final rule publication. Facilities in Agreement States should work with their state radiation control program to understand state-specific implementation timelines.
