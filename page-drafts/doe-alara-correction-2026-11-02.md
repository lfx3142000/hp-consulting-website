# DOE ALARA Requirements and the NRC's 2026 Radiation Protection Proposal: What Is Actually in Effect?

**Correction — November 2, 2026:** An earlier version of this article claimed that DOE removed ALARA from its radiation-protection rules effective January 9, 2026. That claim was not supported by a controlling DOE regulation or directive and should not have been presented as fact. DOE's current Occupational Radiation Protection Program guidance explicitly states that occupational doses governed by 10 CFR 835 must be kept ALARA. We have corrected the article to distinguish DOE's existing requirements from the NRC's separate proposed rulemaking.

## Direct answer

**DOE continues to describe ALARA as a requirement under 10 CFR 835.** The NRC published a **proposed** radiation-protection rule on July 15, 2026 (Docket NRC-2025-1140) that would remove ALARA references from NRC regulations and introduce a graded approach to dose management. A proposal does not change currently effective NRC regulations, Agreement State requirements, or license conditions.

## What DOE requires

DOE's Occupational Radiation Protection Program page states that doses are required to be ALARA and must not exceed the limits in 10 CFR 835. The regulatory text includes a documented radiation protection program and formal plans and measures for applying the ALARA process to occupational exposure. The previous claim of a January 9, 2026 DOE repeal is withdrawn.

## What the NRC proposed in July 2026

On July 15, 2026, NRC published its proposal to modernize radiation-protection regulations, including reconsideration of ALARA and introduction of a graded dose-management framework. The proposal discusses retained numerical dose limits and potential changes to compliance, reporting, and dose management. The July 15 Federal Register notice identified 10 CFR Parts **19, 20, 34, 35, 40, 50, 53, 61, 71 and 72** in its heading. Do not reproduce the older article's list naming Part 150 without separately verifying the amendatory text.

The original comment deadline was **August 31, 2026**, which has passed. Do not invite readers to submit comments under that expired deadline. NRC's rulemaking status and any future effective date must be checked against the latest official docket and Federal Register notices before publication.

## What licensees should do now

Continue following applicable NRC or Agreement State regulations and your license conditions. Do not remove ALARA commitments, change dose-management procedures, or assume new caregiver or worker dose provisions apply merely because a rule has been proposed. Review potential future changes only as a planning exercise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Did DOE eliminate ALARA in January 2026?** No controlling DOE repeal was verified; current DOE guidance still explicitly describes ALARA as required under 10 CFR 835.

**Did NRC eliminate ALARA in July 2026?** No. NRC published a proposal in July 2026. Proposed regulatory text is not effective law.

**Should my facility revise its ALARA program now?** Maintain current obligations and license conditions. Monitor the official rulemaking for a final published rule and effective date before changing compliance procedures.
