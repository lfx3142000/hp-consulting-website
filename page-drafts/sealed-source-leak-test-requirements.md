## Direct answer

Under 10 CFR 35.67, medical-use licensees generally must leak test sealed sources before first use unless a qualifying recent supplier test is documented, and at intervals not to exceed 6 months unless another interval is approved by the NRC or an Agreement State in the Sealed Source and Device Registry. The test must be capable of detecting 185 Bq (0.005 µCi) of radioactive material in the sample. Specific requirements can vary by source type, license condition, and Agreement State regulation, so licensees should verify the requirements that apply to each source.

## Who this is for

This page is for Radiation Safety Officers, EHS professionals, nuclear medicine programs, research laboratories, and other radioactive material licensees that possess sealed sources and need to establish or review leak-test schedules, documentation, and follow-up procedures.

## When this matters

Leak-test requirements should be reviewed when a facility receives a new sealed source, prepares for an inspection, updates its sealed-source inventory, discovers an overdue test, transfers or returns a source, or changes how a source is used or stored.

## Regulatory basis

- **Primary regulation:** 10 CFR 35.67 for sealed sources and brachytherapy sources used under NRC medical-use licenses.
- **Frequency:** Before first use unless a supplier certificate documents a test within 6 months before transfer, and thereafter at intervals not to exceed 6 months unless another interval is approved.
- **Detection capability:** The sample measurement must be capable of detecting 185 Bq (0.005 µCi) of radioactive material.
- **Positive test response:** If removable contamination is 185 Bq (0.005 µCi) or more, the source must immediately be withdrawn from use and appropriately stored, disposed of, or repaired, and the required report must be filed.
- **Jurisdiction note:** Agreement State licensees should verify applicable state regulations, license conditions, and Sealed Source and Device Registry requirements rather than assuming every source follows the same NRC interval.

## Sealed source leak test compliance checklist

1. Maintain a current inventory of sealed sources, including manufacturer, model, serial number, radionuclide, activity, location, and status.
2. Identify the regulation, license condition, or registry requirement that establishes the leak-test interval for each source.
3. Confirm whether a newly received source has a qualifying supplier leak-test certificate before first use.
4. Establish due dates and a tracking method that identifies upcoming tests before they become overdue.
5. Use a leak-test method and measurement system capable of meeting the applicable detection threshold.
6. Document the source identification, test date, test method, measured result, and other records required by the applicable regulation or license.
7. Review results promptly and have a procedure for removing a leaking source from service and completing required notifications or reports.
8. Before using or transferring a source that has been stored without use, verify whether a current leak test is required.
9. Periodically audit the sealed-source inventory against leak-test records to identify missing sources, incomplete records, or overdue tests.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every sealed source has the same 6-month testing interval without checking exemptions, license conditions, or registry requirements.
- Tracking sources in an inventory but not linking the inventory to leak-test due dates.
- Keeping a test result without enough source identification to show which source was tested.
- Failing to verify the required detection capability of the measurement method.
- Treating a stored source as permanently exempt and overlooking testing requirements before later use or transfer.

## FAQ

### How often must sealed sources be leak tested?

For sources subject to 10 CFR 35.67, the general interval is no more than 6 months unless the NRC or an Agreement State has approved another interval in the Sealed Source and Device Registry. Other source types and licenses may be governed by different regulations or license conditions.

### What is the sealed source leak test limit?

Under 10 CFR 35.67, the test must be capable of detecting 185 Bq (0.005 µCi) of radioactive material in the sample. A result at or above that level of removable contamination triggers removal from use and additional required actions.

### Are all sealed sources required to be leak tested?

No. 10 CFR 35.67 includes exemptions for certain short-lived, gaseous, low-activity, and specified other sources. Sources kept in storage and not used may also be exempt from routine testing while stored, but testing may be required before later use or transfer.

### What records should be kept for sealed source leak tests?

Records should meet the applicable regulation and license conditions and clearly connect the result to the tested source. A practical record system should identify the source, test date, result, and enough information to demonstrate that the test and measurement method met applicable requirements.

### Do Agreement State licensees follow NRC leak-test rules?

Agreement States regulate many radioactive material licensees within their jurisdictions and may adopt compatible requirements, but licensees should verify the specific state regulation, license conditions, and source registry requirements that apply. Do not rely on an NRC regulation alone when the state is the licensing authority.
