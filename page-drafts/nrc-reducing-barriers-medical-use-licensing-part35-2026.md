# NRC Proposes Reducing Barriers to Medical Use Licensing — What Hospitals and Medical Facilities Need to Know

**Direct answer:** The NRC has proposed a rule specifically targeting the reduction of licensing barriers for medical use of byproduct material under 10 CFR Part 35. The proposed rule (Docket NRC-2025-1237), published July 20, 2026, is designed to streamline licensing for hospitals, nuclear medicine departments, radiation therapy programs, and radiopharmacies. This complements but is separate from the broader byproduct materials modernization rule (Docket NRC-2025-1205) that focused on Parts 30, 31, 32, 34, and 39. The comment period is currently open.

## Background: Why This Rule Is Being Proposed

The NRC identified that 10 CFR Part 35 — the regulation governing medical use of byproduct material — contains requirements that create unnecessary barriers to medical licensing without proportionate safety benefit. These barriers have been cited as contributing to:

- Delays in obtaining or amending medical use licenses
- Prescriptive requirements that limit adoption of new medical technologies and radiopharmaceuticals
- Administrative burdens on authorized users, authorized medical physicists, and Radiation Safety Committees
- Delays in patient access to diagnostic and therapeutic nuclear medicine procedures

This rulemaking is part of the NRC's wholesale regulatory reform under Executive Order 14300 (May 2025).

---

## Who Is Affected

This proposed rule directly affects:

- **Hospitals and medical centers** with broad scope or specific scope medical use licenses
- **Nuclear medicine departments** using PET/SPECT radiopharmaceuticals, I-131 therapy, and other diagnostic/therapeutic isotopes
- **Radiation therapy programs** using brachytherapy and other sealed source therapies
- **Radiopharmacies** licensed to prepare and distribute radioactive drugs
- **Research institutions** conducting human radiotracer studies under Part 35
- **Authorized Users (AUs) and Authorized Medical Physicists (AMPs)** seeking recognition or changes in scope

## Anticipated Key Changes

While full details continue to emerge as the proposed rule enters the public comment phase, the NRC has indicated the following areas of focus for the Part 35 rulemaking:

### 1. Streamlined Authorization for New Medical Uses
The proposed rule is expected to streamline the process by which licensees can add new authorized uses, radiopharmaceuticals, or treatment modalities to their license without requiring a formal license amendment for every change. This would reduce the licensing correspondence burden for facilities that regularly adopt new FDA-approved radiopharmaceuticals.

### 2. Authorized User and Authorized Medical Physicist Approval
Current Part 35 requirements for adding or removing Authorized Users and Authorized Medical Physicists can require formal NRC or state approval, even for routine personnel changes. The proposed rule may streamline these processes, particularly for facilities with a robust radiation safety infrastructure and active Radiation Safety Committees.

### 3. Modernized Training and Experience Requirements
The NRC is expected to revisit training and experience (T&E) requirements for Authorized Users across medical use categories (10 CFR 35.100 through 35.600 series), potentially updating these requirements to better reflect modern medical education, residency programs, and board certification pathways.

### 4. Technology-Neutral Requirements
Current Part 35 regulations include technology-specific requirements that can create barriers for emerging radiopharmaceutical technologies, including targeted alpha therapies, theranostic agents, and novel PET tracers. The proposed rule is expected to move toward more technology-neutral standards that facilitate adoption of new treatments without requiring individual exemptions.

### 5. Alignment With Other Proposed Rules
This rule is coordinated with:
- **Reforming and Modernizing the NRC's Radiation Protection Framework** (NRC-2025-1140) — which introduces new caregiver dose limits applicable to radiation therapy patients under Part 20 that work alongside Part 35 patient release regulations.
- **Modernizing NRC Regulations for Byproduct Material Use** (NRC-2025-1205) — which revised decommissioning financial assurance thresholds for short-lived isotopes commonly used in nuclear medicine.

## Impact on Radiation Safety Programs at Medical Facilities

For Radiation Safety Officers (RSOs) and Radiation Safety Committees at medical facilities, this proposed rule may:

- **Reduce amendment frequency**: Fewer formal amendments may be required for routine program changes such as adding new isotopes or modifying therapy procedures within the scope of the license.
- **Change training documentation requirements**: RSOs should monitor proposed changes to T&E documentation requirements for authorized users and AMPs.
- **Affect patient release criteria**: In combination with the Part 20 caregiver dose limit changes from the radiation protection framework rule, facilities will need to review patient release procedures for radiation therapy patients.
- **Affect Radiation Safety Committee procedures**: If AU/AMP approval processes are modified, the RSC's role in approving additions or changes may be affected.

## Agreement State Medical Licensees

All six New England states (Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine) are NRC Agreement States. Medical use licensees in these states are regulated by their state radiation control program, not directly by the NRC.

New England Agreement States that regulate medical use include:
- **Massachusetts**: DPH Radiation Control Program
- **Connecticut**: DPH Radiation Division
- **Rhode Island**: DEM Office of Air and Radiation

Agreement States must adopt compatible regulations within 3 years of NRC final rule publication. Until state adoption, existing state-specific medical use regulations remain in effect.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does this rule change my existing medical use license?**
Not immediately. Proposed rules require public comment, NRC review, and a final rule publication before they take effect. Your existing license conditions remain in effect until the final rule is adopted.

**Will I need to revise my Radiation Safety Program or RSC procedures?**
Possibly, depending on the final rule content. Key areas to watch include AU/AMP approval processes, ALARA program procedures (which will also be affected by the radiation protection framework rule), and patient release criteria. Advantage Health Physics can review your program and advise on needed updates once the final rule is published.

**How does this interact with the ALARA removal proposed rule?**
The ALARA removal rule (NRC-2025-1140) also amends 10 CFR Part 35 by introducing new caregiver and member-of-the-public dose limits for radiation therapy patients. The Part 35 medical use licensing rule (NRC-2025-1237) focuses on licensing process barriers. Both rules will need to be read together once finalized.

**Can I comment on this proposed rule?**
Yes. Comments are accepted at regulations.gov using Docket ID NRC-2025-1237. Advantage Health Physics can assist medical facilities in preparing informed public comments based on your facility's specific licensing circumstances.
