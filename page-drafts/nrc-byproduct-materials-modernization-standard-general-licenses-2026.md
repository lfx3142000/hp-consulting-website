# NRC Proposes Sweeping Changes to Byproduct Material Licensing — What Licensees Need to Know

**Direct answer:** The NRC has proposed major changes to its regulations governing the use of byproduct material in medicine, research, and industry (10 CFR Parts 30, 31, 32, 34, 39, 40, 70, and 150). The proposed rule (Docket NRC-2025-1205), published May 18, 2026, would create a new class of "standard general licenses" (SGLs), raise the thresholds for decommissioning financial assurance obligations, streamline reporting requirements, and modernize rules for industrial radiography, well logging, and microsource distribution. The public comment period closed July 2, 2026. A final rule is expected by November 2026.

## Background: Why These Changes Are Being Proposed

This rulemaking is part of the NRC's wholesale regulatory reform under Executive Order 14300 ("Ordering the Reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission," May 2025). The NRC identified requirements within its byproduct materials regulations that it determined were outdated, overly prescriptive, or not commensurate with actual radiological risk.

The NRC's stated goal is to:
- Streamline the licensing process for low-risk activities
- Reduce administrative and compliance burden on licensees
- Better align requirements to actual risk levels
- Remove anticompetitive barriers in the materials licensing framework

The proposed changes affect NRC-regulated facilities and, once adopted by Agreement States, will also apply to Agreement State licensees.

---

## Key Change #1: New Standard General Licenses (SGLs)

The most significant structural change is the creation of **five technology-specific Standard General Licenses (SGLs)** under a new 10 CFR Part 31 Subpart C.

A general license is granted by regulation, without requiring an individual license application. SGLs would be available for:

| SGL Category | Example Uses |
|---|---|
| Fixed gauges | Density, level, or moisture gauges permanently installed at industrial sites |
| Portable gauges | Moisture/density gauges used in construction and geotechnical work |
| Certain medical uses | Specific in-vitro testing, calibration |
| Analytical equipment | X-ray fluorescence, electron capture detectors |
| In-vitro testing | Clinical and laboratory testing with prepackaged radioactive kits |

**How SGLs differ from specific licenses:**
- SGLs require **registration** with the NRC (not a full application)
- SGLs have **lower application and annual fees**
- No amendment requests for routine program changes
- SGL holders must keep material inventories **below thresholds** that would trigger specific licensing requirements
- SGL holders remain **subject to NRC inspections** comparable to specific license holders

Entities currently holding specific licenses in these categories may elect to convert to an SGL or remain specifically licensed.

---

## Key Change #2: Revised Decommissioning Financial Assurance (DFA) Thresholds

The proposed rule would substantially revise decommissioning financial assurance requirements under 10 CFR Parts 30 and 70 by:

- **Replacing the current Part 30 Appendix B values** (which trigger DFA obligations and decommissioning funding plans) with generally less restrictive values from **Part 20 Appendix C**.
- **Eliminating DFA requirements for all radionuclides with half-lives of 120 days or less.** This is significant for medical licensees using short-lived isotopes such as Tc-99m, I-123, and F-18.
- **Adding radionuclides** not currently in Appendix B that are associated with emerging medical and industrial technologies.
- **Providing specific possession values** for naturally occurring and NARM (Naturally Occurring and Accelerator-Produced Radioactive Material) radionuclides, so medical licensees using these isotopes will not have to calculate custom DFA requirements.

**Who benefits most:** Medical licensees, research institutions, and industrial users who hold or plan to apply for licenses involving isotopes that are currently listed in Appendix B at overly conservative thresholds.

---

## Key Change #3: Increased In-Vitro Radionuclide Limits (Section 31.11)

The proposed rule would increase the prepackaged quantity limits for general licensed in-vitro clinical and laboratory testing materials:

| Radionuclide | Current Limit | Proposed Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon-14 (C-14) | 10 μCi | 100 μCi |
| Selenium-75 (Se-75) | 10 μCi | 100 μCi |
| Cobalt-57 (Co-57) | 10 μCi | 100 μCi |
| Hydrogen-3 / Tritium (H-3) | 50 μCi | 1,000 μCi |

These changes would allow some facilities currently operating under a specific license to transition to a general license, reducing regulatory burden and costs.

---

## Key Change #4: Streamlined Reporting Requirements

The proposed rule would eliminate several annual reporting requirements that the NRC determined impose burden without commensurate safety benefit:

- **Exempt product distribution reports** under Sections 32.11, 32.14, 32.22, 32.26, 32.30, and 40.52 (e.g., for smoke detectors containing Am-241, self-luminous products, tritium exit signs) would no longer require annual transfer reports to the NRC.
- Additional reporting simplifications for Agreement State reciprocity under Part 150.

---

## Key Change #5: Modernization of Industrial Radiography (Part 34)

The proposed revisions to 10 CFR Part 34 would:
- Reduce administrative requirements for industrial radiographic licensees.
- Remove anticompetitive barriers in radiography equipment qualification and personnel certification.
- Modernize recordkeeping and operational requirements to better reflect current industry practices.

Industrial radiography licensees should review the proposed rule to identify specific changes affecting camera usage, survey requirements, and crew requirements.

---

## Key Change #6: Microsource Distribution Pathways (Part 32)

The proposed rule would expand regulatory pathways for the preparation and distribution of **radioactive microsources**, including radioactive microspheres used in medical procedures such as intravascular brachytherapy. Commercial radiopharmacies and other authorized applicants would be able to manufacture and distribute microsources under the same framework used for radioactive drugs under Section 32.72.

---

## Key Change #7: Well Logging Modernization (Part 39)

The proposed rule includes modernization of well logging regulations under 10 CFR Part 39, updating requirements to better reflect current well logging technologies, equipment, and operational practices.

---

## Agreement State Licensees

New England states (Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine) are all NRC Agreement States. The proposed rule, once finalized, would not automatically apply to Agreement State licensees. Agreement States must adopt compatible regulations within 3 years of NRC final rule publication.

NRC has also published interim staff guidance alongside the proposed rule:
- *Guidance for the Implementation of 10 CFR Part 31 Subpart C Standard General Licenses*
- *Guidance on Reciprocity*

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert my specific license to a Standard General License?**
Entities operating fixed gauges, portable gauges, certain analytical equipment, and qualifying in-vitro testing programs may be eligible to convert to an SGL once the rule is finalized. You would register with the NRC rather than hold a specific license. Advantage Health Physics can evaluate your current license and advise whether conversion makes sense for your facility.

**Do I still need decommissioning financial assurance if I use Tc-99m, F-18, or I-123?**
Under the proposed rule, radionuclides with half-lives of 120 days or less would be removed from DFA consideration. Tc-99m (6-hour half-life), F-18 (110-minute half-life), and I-123 (13-hour half-life) would all qualify for elimination from DFA requirements.

**Does this rule change my existing specific license?**
No. The proposed rule does not automatically modify existing specific licenses. Existing licensees may elect to transition to an SGL or remain specifically licensed. Some requirements (such as annual reporting for exempt product distributors) would be eliminated for all affected licensees once the rule is finalized.

**When will Agreement States adopt these changes?**
Agreement States have up to 3 years from the NRC final rule publication to adopt compatible regulations. The final rule is expected November 2026, placing the state adoption deadline around November 2029. However, states may adopt sooner.

**My facility distributes exempt consumer products containing small amounts of radioactive material. Does this affect my annual transfer reports?**
Yes. If finalized, this proposed rule would eliminate the annual transfer reporting requirement for distributors of products under Sections 32.11, 32.14, 32.22, 32.26, 32.30, and 40.52.
